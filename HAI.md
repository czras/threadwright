# Human–Agent Interaction

> **Synthesis**
>
> This document synthesizes the research currently represented by
> [RESEARCH-0005](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0005_human%23U2013agent-collaboration-and-the-conceptual-territory-of-threadwright.md)
> and the [RESEARCH-0006 corpus](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/).
>
> It is a derived synthesis, not a replacement for the underlying research and not Threadwright doctrine.

## 1. Purpose

[RESEARCH-0005](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0005_human%23U2013agent-collaboration-and-the-conceptual-territory-of-threadwright.md) began from a question about what happens when a human and an AI agent participate in an ongoing pursuit in which interaction can change not only the actions taken, but the human's understanding, capability, and potentially the intention being pursued.

[RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) was created as an adversarial investigation of that territory against the existing Human–Agent Interaction (HAI) research corpus.

Read together, the two researches produce a useful boundary:

> Much of the apparently new conceptual territory is already populated by established research. The more interesting remaining problem is how existing mechanisms can be composed into human-agent systems that optimize present effectiveness while also preserving or developing future human capability.

This document records that boundary and the conceptual connections that survive it.

## 2. What RESEARCH-0005 contributed

[RESEARCH-0005](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0005_human%23U2013agent-collaboration-and-the-conceptual-territory-of-threadwright.md) explored human–agent collaboration as a potentially multi-dimensional problem.

The initial productivity question expanded into three simultaneous dimensions:

1. **Present-task performance** — accomplishing the current task effectively.
2. **Learning** — developing understanding and capability through the pursuit.
3. **Autonomous capability** — increasing the human's ability to pursue, evaluate, and regulate future work independently.

This led to several distinctions:

- task performance is not equivalent to learning;
- cognitive offloading is not inherently harmful;
- the relevant question is which cognitive functions are delegated and whether they are part of the capability being developed or maintained;
- persistent collaborative interaction differs from one-shot cognitive offloading;
- AI assistance may either support capability development or substitute for the activity through which capability develops.

[RESEARCH-0005](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0005_human%23U2013agent-collaboration-and-the-conceptual-territory-of-threadwright.md) then widened the problem from the individual to the **expertise pipeline**: if AI changes the activities through which novices become competent practitioners, it may change how professional expertise is reproduced.

It also introduced the idea of **adaptive collaborative pursuit**: a human and agent pursue an intention while feedback from the pursuit can change how the intention is pursued and, when warranted, the intention itself.

These ideas were deliberately treated as research hypotheses rather than Threadwright doctrine.

## 3. What RESEARCH-0006 changed

[RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) did not attempt to confirm [RESEARCH-0005](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0005_human%23U2013agent-collaboration-and-the-conceptual-territory-of-threadwright.md).

Its explicit purpose was adversarial:

> investigate how the research community has already tested, refined, rejected, or reframed the emerging ideas.

The corpus reconstruction shows that many of the broad claims do not survive as novelty claims.

The HAI literature already contains substantial work concerning:

- human-agent collaboration;
- mutual adaptation and co-learning;
- dynamic agency allocation;
- changing goals and goal negotiation;
- problem reframing;
- sensemaking;
- reciprocal knowledge exchange;
- dependency and deskilling;
- human-AI teaming;
- epistemic agency;
- capability and training;
- scaffolding and apprenticeship.

The research therefore repeatedly **kills weak novelty claims** rather than accumulating them.

This is an important result in itself.

## 4. The surviving intersection

Across the corpus, one intersection remains particularly useful:

> **Human-agent interaction in which immediate task performance and the future capability of the human participant are treated as simultaneous concerns.**

The broad mechanisms already exist separately.

The unresolved question is how they interact when AI assistance becomes persistent, adaptive, and increasingly capable.

This changes the research objective from:

> Find a novel theory.

toward:

> **Find the smallest sufficient set of established concepts and mechanisms needed to engineer the desired class of human-agent systems.**

[RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) explicitly identifies this as a possible **composition problem**.

## 5. From adaptation to adaptive pursuit

The research initially treated mutual adaptation as potentially novel.

The corpus eliminated that claim.

Human-agent mutual adaptation, co-learning, dynamic agency allocation, adaptation to goals, goal negotiation, and fluid collaboration all have substantial prior art.

A more precise distinction survived:

> **Adaptation of pursuit is not necessarily the same thing as adaptation of intent.**

Even this required further qualification. [RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) found prior art for dynamic goals, problem reframing, objective revision, sensemaking, and human-AI co-construction.

The remaining conceptual question is therefore narrower:

> Can execution, strategy, agency allocation, problem representation, objective refinement, and deeper intent transformation be represented as parts of one coupled process across time?

The research does not establish that such a unified model is novel.

It establishes that the existing concepts are distributed across several research traditions and that their composition may be worth investigating.

## 6. Pursuit as an epistemically generative process

One particularly important finding survived several rounds of adversarial review:

> **Pursuing an intention can generate knowledge that changes what should be pursued.**

But the broad proposition is not novel. [RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) found prior art in epistemic action, reflective practice, design research, sensemaking, and human-AI co-construction.

The useful remaining distinction concerns the role of the **pursuit itself**.

The pursuit is not merely an execution mechanism for a previously fixed objective.

It can produce observations, surprises, failures, interpretations, and evidence that change the representation of the problem and consequently the objective.

This connects directly to Threadwright's emphasis on reassessment:

```text
intention
    ↓
pursuit
    ↓
observations / evidence
    ↓
changed understanding
    ↓
changed pursuit
```

The loop may therefore change not only the map of the territory, but what the participants believe is worth pursuing.

## 7. Epistemic agency and capability

[RESEARCH-0006/006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/006_epistemic-agency+capability.md) establishes another important distinction.

Epistemic agency is already a well-developed research concept. Human-AI systems can involve shared epistemic agency, and AI can influence or relocate aspects of cognitive governance.

The more consequential distinction is:

Agency preservation — the human remains capable of governing the epistemic process.

versus:

Capability development — the human becomes progressively better at governing the epistemic process.

These are not equivalent.

A human can remain the responsible decision-maker in an AI-assisted interaction without becoming more capable through that interaction.

Conversely, an interaction can be deliberately designed so that assistance contributes to capability development.

This produces a central surviving question:

Under what interaction conditions does AI assistance produce capability development rather than capability substitution?

## 8. Capability development is a different time scale

[RESEARCH-0006/007](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/007_capability-development.md) shows that existing learning science already provides substantial machinery for capability development:

self-regulated learning;
cognitive apprenticeship;
scaffolding and fading;
deliberate practice;
generation and retrieval;
desirable difficulties;
feedback;
adaptive expertise;
transfer.

These mechanisms imply that immediate performance is not sufficient to evaluate development.

A useful distinction is:

current interaction
       │
       ├──► current task performance
       │
       └──► capability trajectory
                  │
                  ▼
             future performance

AI can improve the first branch while weakening the second, or potentially improve both.

The developmental value of an activity is therefore not identical to its immediate productivity.

This is why cognitive offloading is not uniform. Some delegated work may be incidental overhead. Other delegated work may remove productive cognitive practice.

The research does not establish a universal classification of which activities should or should not be delegated.

It establishes the need to reason about the capability objective and the developmental mechanism.

## 9. The expertise pipeline

The individual capability problem scales.

Professional expertise is reproduced through a process involving practice, feedback, increasing competence, independent work, and eventually mentoring.

AI substitution could therefore alter the mechanism by which expertise is reproduced.

[RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) identifies three broad possibilities:

Negative mechanism

AI performs activities that previously constituted productive practice, reducing opportunities for capability development.

Positive mechanism

AI provides individualized scaffolding, feedback, challenge, and support that accelerate capability development.

Transformation mechanism

AI changes apprenticeship itself: practitioners spend less time executing routine work and more time practicing judgment, evaluation, adaptation, and deciding when and how to delegate.

The research does not establish which trajectory will dominate.

It establishes that the trajectory itself is a legitimate object of study.

## 10. Connection to Threadwright

The research does not prove Threadwright.

Instead, it helps explain why several Threadwright ideas are relevant to this research territory.

### Persistent artifacts

If learning, evidence, decisions, rejected paths, and changed interpretations matter to future action, then preserving them makes the trajectory inspectable.

### Reassessment

If pursuit can generate knowledge that changes the problem representation or objective, then a fixed plan is insufficient. The system needs repeated reassessment.

### Exploration

If intelligent machines lower the cost of cognitive exploration, more branches become economically reachable.

### Epistemic integrity

If AI can generate fluent proposals, interpretations, and conclusions, the system must distinguish what was observed, inferred, hypothesized, proposed, decided, and validated.

### Human judgment

Existing research does not justify replacing human judgment with a deterministic interaction recipe. Capability, context, consequences, expertise, and objectives remain relevant.

These connections should be understood as research-informed relationships, not as claims that the HAI corpus validates the Threadwright methodology as a whole.

## 11. The deeper common structure

Read together, the researches point toward a common structure:

current pursuit
      ↓
interaction with environment / agent
      ↓
observations and evidence
      ↓
changed knowledge
      ↓
changed capability and/or agency
      ↓
changed understanding of the pursuit
      ↓
reassessment
      ↓
next pursuit

Several levels can change during this process:

activity
   ↓
strategy
   ↓
agency allocation
   ↓
problem representation
   ↓
objective
   ↓
possibly deeper intent

The HAI corpus already contains research on many of these levels independently.

The unresolved engineering question is whether they can be composed into a practical long-horizon human-agent system without losing the mechanisms that produce human capability.

## 12. Current research boundary

The combined research therefore supports a deliberately modest position.

### Established or strongly supported

Human-agent adaptation is established prior art.
Dynamic agency and co-learning are established research areas.
Goal negotiation, objective revision, reframing, and sensemaking have substantial prior art.
Epistemic agency is an established concept.
Human-AI collaboration can require training.
Capability development has substantial established scientific machinery.
Immediate task performance and long-term capability are distinct outcomes.

### Still open

How should these mechanisms be composed into one operational human-agent system?
Under what conditions does assistance become substitution?
Which activities should remain practiced by the human when future capability is an explicit objective?
How should assistance adapt as human capability changes?
How should capability trajectories be evaluated over months or years?
What distinguishes objective refinement from deeper intent transformation?
Does persistent AI-mediated pursuit change the human's ability to recognize when the current objective deserves reconsideration?

These are not claims that the existing literature has no answers.

They are the surviving questions after the adversarial review performed in [RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/).

## 13. Relation to the L5 README

The README presents Threadwright at a much higher level:

Threadwright is about charting the unknown.

It describes exploration, observation, learning, map updating, reassessment, possibility, and the economics of exploration.

This document provides the research context beneath that compressed model.

In particular, it explains why:

interaction can be treated as more than one-shot task completion;
learning and capability matter alongside immediate performance;
pursuit can generate knowledge that changes subsequent pursuit;
persistent knowledge matters across time;
reassessment is necessary when the evaluator or objective changes;
and the human-agent relationship should be studied as a trajectory rather than as isolated prompts and answers.

The README is therefore the compressed orientation.

This document is the conceptual bridge.

The underlying research remains the evidentiary and adversarial layer.

## 14. Research discipline

The strongest conclusion of [RESEARCH-0006](methodology/artifacts/research/RESEARCH-0006_human-agent-interaction-corpus-reconstruction-and-adversarial-review/) is methodological:

We probably aren't the first people to think about this.

That should remain a standing constraint on future work.

When a new concept appears:

reconstruct the relevant prior art;
attempt to eliminate the novelty claim;
distinguish established concepts from partial overlaps;
preserve contradictions and unresolved questions;
identify exactly what, if anything, remains;
only then consider whether Threadwright needs a new concept or mechanism.

The purpose is not to prove that Threadwright is novel.

The purpose is to determine what the existing body of knowledge already explains and what remains worth building or investigating.
