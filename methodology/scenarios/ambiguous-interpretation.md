# Ambiguous Interpretation

**Heuristic:** H9 — Generate Broadly, Compress Deliberately

## Context

A stakeholder provides ambiguous or incomplete intent. A knowledge gap has several possible explanations. Prematurely choosing one interpretation or explanation could lead to solving the wrong problem or optimizing around an incorrect assumption.

## Generation

Generate multiple plausible interpretations of the intent or explanations of the gap.

For each, identify:

- implied outcome;
- assumptions it depends on;
- constraints it implies;
- unresolved questions;
- evidence supporting it;
- what observation would distinguish it from alternatives.

Do not immediately converge on a single interpretation. Allow competing framings to coexist long enough to evaluate which uncertainty is most consequential.

## Observation

Broad generation may expose:

- ambiguity that was previously hidden;
- conflicting assumptions;
- alternative desired outcomes;
- different interpretations of constraints;
- questions that materially affect the solution;
- previously unconsidered explanations;
- observations that would distinguish several hypotheses simultaneously.

## Compression

Group equivalent interpretations or hypotheses. Remove those that are clearly unsupported or inconsequential.

Retain the smallest set that represents materially different possibilities. Identify which remaining uncertainty is consequential and which is not.

## Next Action

Use the resulting interpretation or hypothesis set to determine the most effective next knowledge-generating action. This may be:

- asking the stakeholder a clarifying question;
- researching existing knowledge;
- testing an assumption;
- building a prototype;
- running an experiment;
- observing the relevant context.

## Evaluation

Compare with immediately selecting the most plausible interpretation or hypothesis.

Evaluate:

- ambiguity discovered;
- premature assumptions avoided;
- cost of exploration;
- consequential uncertainty reduced;
- quality of subsequent decisions.

## Question

> Does broad generation of interpretations reduce the risk of premature convergence sufficiently to justify the additional evaluation cost?
