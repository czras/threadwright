# RESEARCH-0006: Human-Agent Interaction Corpus Reconstruction and Adversarial Review

## Status

Research in progress.

This document defines a dedicated investigation of the established **Human-Agent Interaction (HAI)** research corpus.

It is a research artifact, not Threadwright doctrine.

This research branch was created as a consequence of RESEARCH-0005 discovering that Human-Agent Interaction is not merely an adjacent collection of research, but a long-running international research community with a substantial historical corpus.

The purpose of RESEARCH-0006 is to reconstruct that corpus, understand its conceptual evolution, identify established prior art, find empirical evidence and counterevidence relevant to RESEARCH-0005, and identify genuine unresolved questions.

The purpose is explicitly **adversarial**.

The desired outcome is not validation of RESEARCH-0005.

The desired outcome is to discover where RESEARCH-0005 is wrong, redundant, already established, insufficiently precise, or genuinely incomplete.

# 2. Why HAI Deserves a Dedicated Investigation

Human-Agent Interaction is an established interdisciplinary research field.

The international HAI conference lineage began in 2013 and HAI 2026 is the 14th International Conference.

The associated Japanese HAI Symposium lineage extends substantially further, with publicly accessible material reaching back to 2006.

This creates a longitudinal corpus spanning roughly two decades.

The research community has investigated many topics directly relevant to RESEARCH-0005, including:

- human-agent interaction;
- agency;
- autonomy;
- human-agent collaboration;
- human-agent teaming;
- delegation;
- trust;
- adaptation;
- initiative;
- learning;
- dependency;
- skill development;
- capability;
- reciprocal knowledge exchange;
- conversational agents;
- social interaction;
- agent memory;
- LLM-based agents;
- and societal consequences.

The corpus therefore provides an unusually valuable opportunity:

> **Instead of asking whether an idea sounds plausible, investigate how the research community has already tested, refined, rejected, or reframed it.**

# 8. Adversarial Review Framework

Every RESEARCH-0005 hypothesis encountered in the HAI corpus should be classified.

### Category A — Already established

The concept exists clearly in prior research.

Action:

> Remove novelty claim.

### Category B — Established but differently framed

Existing research addresses substantially the same phenomenon using different terminology.

Action:

> Determine whether the existing terminology is sufficient.

### Category C — Partial overlap

Existing work addresses a component but not the complete relationship proposed by RESEARCH-0005.

Action:

> Identify exactly what is missing.

### Category D — Contradicted

Evidence directly challenges the hypothesis.

Action:

> Record the contradiction and reassess the hypothesis.

### Category E — Unresolved

Research identifies the problem but evidence is inconclusive.

Action:

> Preserve as open research question.

### Category F — Apparently underexplored

Very little relevant research is found.

Action:

> Treat as candidate research territory, not as proof of novelty.

The last category requires particularly careful handling.

> **Absence from the corpus is not proof of absence from the literature.**

# 9. Current Known Adversarial Findings

The first exploratory pass has already produced several important results.

## Human-agent collaboration

Already extensively researched.

No novelty claim survives.

## Agency + interaction + adaptation

Already explicitly conceptualized in human-AI collaboration research.

No simple novelty claim survives.

## Delegation

Established research territory.

The important remaining question concerns delegation under multiple objectives, including future human capability.

## Dependency

Established research territory.

The unresolved question is whether dependency interacts with long-term expertise reproduction.

## Skill development / deskilling

Established research territory.

Evidence does not support a simple:

> AI use → deskilling

causal rule.

Effects appear to depend on task, interaction structure, training and context.

## Training human-agent teams

Established research territory.

Research has investigated scaffolded versus self-paced approaches and human strategies for deciding when to leverage agents.

## Reciprocal knowledge exchange

An emerging established research direction.

The concept of humans and agents mutually eliciting and providing knowledge is already present in HAI.

## Thinking partner / cognitive partner

Established conceptual neighborhood.

"Thinking companion" therefore cannot be treated as a novel concept merely because the phrase is convenient.

# 10. The Surviving Question

The first adversarial pass leaves a narrower question alive:

> **What determines whether human-agent interaction produces capability development rather than capability substitution?**

A stronger formulation adds the optimization problem:

> **How should cognitive work be allocated when present task performance and future human capability are simultaneous objectives?**

This is the current primary candidate for deeper investigation.

It should be tested against the full HAI corpus.

# 13. The Judgment / JPÉ Hypothesis

RESEARCH-0005 currently contains a deliberately informal intuition:

> "AI can do lots of things but not JPÉ."

This wording must not become a research conclusion.

The research question is instead:

> **Which human capabilities are comparatively difficult to transfer, formalize, elicit, reproduce, or train into artificial agents?**

Possible candidate characteristics include:

- tacit knowledge;
- situated knowledge;
- contextual interpretation;
- common-sense reasoning;
- judgment;
- value-sensitive decisions;
- recognition of unstated constraints;
- knowing what matters;
- recognizing when a technically correct solution is inappropriate.

The investigation should search specifically for research that:

1. supports this distinction;
2. contradicts it;
3. operationalizes it;
4. demonstrates successful transfer of apparently tacit capabilities;
5. identifies alternative explanations.

The resulting conclusion may be:

- the hypothesis is wrong;
- the capability is transferable;
- the capability is difficult but increasingly transferable;
- the capability remains difficult under current systems;
- or the distinction is better explained by existing expertise/tacit-knowledge theory.

Any of these is a successful research outcome.


# 16. Persistent versus Transactional Interaction

RESEARCH-0005 distinguishes:

```text
persistent collaboration
```

from:

```text
one-shot delegation
```

This distinction must be tested against existing HAI research.

The investigation should determine whether persistent interaction produces qualitatively different:

- learning;
- trust;
- calibration;
- dependency;
- agency;
- adaptation;
- knowledge exchange;
- capability outcomes.

However, persistent interaction must not be assumed superior.

A one-shot delegation can be exactly correct when learning is not part of the objective.

The relevant variable may instead be:

> **purpose + task + capability objective + interaction structure.**

# 17. Adaptive Collaborative Pursuit

RESEARCH-0005 proposes:

> **Adaptive collaborative pursuit** — a human and agent collaborate in pursuing an intention while feedback from the pursuit can change both how the intention is pursued and, when warranted, the intention itself.

The HAI corpus should test whether this is:

1. already described by an existing concept;
2. a combination of existing concepts;
3. a useful synthesis;
4. or genuinely underexplored.

# 22. Current Known Research Leads

The first exploratory pass identified several particularly relevant lines of investigation.

### Agency / Interaction / Adaptation

A 2024 framework explicitly decomposes human-AI collaboration into these dimensions.

This should be examined closely because it may subsume part of the emerging adaptive-collaborative-pursuit concept.

### Human dependency

HAI has explicitly investigated human-agent dependency.

This should be compared with the RESEARCH-0005 dependency loop.

### Skill development / deskilling

HAI research has investigated prolonged AI assistant use and possible deskilling.

The evidence should be examined carefully rather than summarized as "AI causes deskilling."

### Scaffolded human-agent training

HAI research has compared scaffolded and self-paced training of human-agent teams.

This is directly relevant to capability development and selective delegation.

### Capabilities and constraints

HAI research has investigated modelling what human and AI agents can and cannot change.

This may provide existing theoretical machinery relevant to human/agent capability allocation.

### Reciprocal knowledge elicitation

HAI workshops investigate mutual knowledge elicitation between humans and agents.

This is potentially relevant to the thinking-companion and practitioner-knowledge hypotheses.

These are **research leads, not conclusions**.

# 25. Current Working Thesis

At the beginning of this investigation, RESEARCH-0005 appeared to be exploring an emerging conceptual space around human-agent collaboration.

The first HAI corpus reconnaissance changes that interpretation.

The broad territory is already well populated.

The potentially interesting question is narrower:

> **What happens when human-agent collaboration is explicitly treated as a system whose objectives include not only accomplishing the current task, but developing and preserving the future capability of the human participant?**

And at the societal level:

> **What happens when that capability-development problem is repeated across the population and therefore changes the mechanism by which professional expertise is reproduced?**

Whether these questions represent genuine research gaps remains to be determined by this investigation.
