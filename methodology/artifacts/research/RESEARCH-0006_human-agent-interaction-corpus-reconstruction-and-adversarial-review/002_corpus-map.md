# RESEARCH-0006: Human-Agent Interaction Corpus

## 002 — Corpus Map

### Status

This document reconstructs the research territory surrounding Human-Agent Interaction (HAI).

It is a **corpus-mapping artifact**, not an adversarial review and not a statement about what Threadwright should become.

The purpose is to establish:

- what the HAI research community considers Human-Agent Interaction;
- where the field came from;
- how the field has evolved;
- which conceptual areas have become established;
- which concepts relevant to RESEARCH-0005 already have substantial prior art;
- which areas warrant deeper corpus mining.

---

# 1. Corpus Boundary

The HAI corpus is broader than the international HAI conference series.

The international conference began with the **First International Conference on Human-Agent Interaction in 2013**.

However, the Japanese **HAI Symposium** began in 2006 and continued as a distinct research lineage. The HAI community's own historical material identifies the 2006 symposium as the first HAI symposium and maintains an archive of the subsequent symposiums.

Therefore the corpus is divided into tiers according to their role in reconstruction.

## Tier A — Primary Corpus

### International HAI Conference

**2013–present**

This is the primary corpus for systematic reconstruction.

It contains the international HAI conference series from the first conference in 2013 through HAI 2026.

Primary corpus questions include:

- What concepts does the international HAI community use?
- How have those concepts evolved?
- What problems have repeatedly appeared?
- Which concepts became established?
- Which questions remain contested or unresolved?

---

## Tier B — Ancestral Corpus

### Japanese HAI Symposium

**2006–2012 initially**

The Japanese HAI Symposium is treated as the **ancestral layer** of the corpus.

Its purpose is not initially to receive the same depth of analysis as Tier A.

Instead, it is used to answer:

> **Which concepts, questions, and assumptions existed before the international HAI conference was established?**

The initial focus is therefore the **2006–2012 pre-international period**.

The later Japanese symposium lineage may be consulted when necessary, particularly where it provides continuity or additional evidence for concepts appearing in the international conference corpus.

The ancestral corpus is especially valuable for adversarial purposes.

A concept discovered in HAI 2025 that appears novel may look very different if the same conceptual territory was already being explored in HAI 2007.

---

## Tier C — Adjacent Corpus

Research outside the HAI conference/symposium lineage.

Examples include:

- human-computer interaction;
- human-robot interaction;
- human-AI collaboration;
- hybrid intelligence;
- cognitive science;
- educational technology;
- organizational science;
- automation and skill research;
- human factors;
- adaptive control;
- distributed cognition;
- AI safety and alignment where directly relevant.

Tier C is **lead-driven**.

It should be expanded when the HAI corpus points toward an external research tradition that is necessary to understand a concept.

It is not an unrestricted literature search.

---

## Tier D — Opportunistic Evidence

Individual papers, books, datasets, theories, experiments, or other sources encountered outside the defined corpus.

Tier D material is used only when it materially affects interpretation.

It should not silently become part of the main corpus.

---

# 2. Corpus Strategy

The corpus is therefore:

```text
RESEARCH-0006
│
├── Tier A — International HAI
│   └── 2013 → present
│       systematic reconstruction
│
├── Tier B — Japanese HAI Symposium
│   └── 2006 → 2012
│       ancestral reconstruction
│
├── Tier C — Adjacent research
│   └── lead-driven investigation
│
└── Tier D — Opportunistic evidence
    └── used only when materially relevant
```

This prevents two opposite errors.

### Error 1 — Artificially narrow corpus

Starting in 2013 could cause earlier HAI concepts to be mistaken for innovations of the international conference era.

### Error 2 — Unbounded corpus

Attempting to reconstruct every neighboring research field would turn RESEARCH-0006 into an uncontrolled literature review.

The tiering preserves historical depth while keeping the research tractable.

# 3. Foundational Boundary

The first international HAI conference already defined a broad interdisciplinary territory.

Its stated scope included interaction with:

- robots;
- software agents;
- digitally mediated human-human communication;
- embodied agents;
- learning agents;
- agents capable of influencing humans;
- theoretical models of HAI;
- relationships between different interactions;
- experimental analysis.

The founding conference therefore establishes an important baseline:

> Human-Agent Interaction was conceived as a field concerned with more than interface mechanics.

Questions about learning, influence, adaptation, relationships, agency, and human behavior were present from the beginning.

This makes the founding material a high-value anchor for later conceptual comparisons.

# 4. Historical Reconstruction

The preliminary historical map suggests several broad phases.

These are **navigation hypotheses**, not yet established historical conclusions.

## 4.1 Prehistory / Formation

### 2006–2012

The Japanese HAI Symposium establishes HAI as an identifiable research area before the international conference exists.

Primary research question:

> What did the early HAI community mean by “Human-Agent Interaction”?

Questions for the ancestral scan:

- Which concepts recur?
- Which concepts appear only once?
- What theoretical assumptions were already present?
- Which later international-conference concepts can be traced backward?
- What disappeared?
- What survived?

---

## 4.2 Field Formation

### 2013–2015

The international conference establishes HAI as an interdisciplinary field spanning different forms of artificial and mediated agents.

Primary question:

> What common research problem is shared by apparently different agent-interaction settings?

---

## 4.3 Learning, Adaptation, and Autonomy

### 2016–2017

The corpus increasingly addresses learning, social intelligence, autonomy, and adaptive behavior.

Primary question:

> What changes when the agent is no longer a fixed interaction partner?

# 5. Conceptual Terrain

The preliminary corpus can be organized into recurring conceptual clusters.

| Cluster | Core concern |
|---|---|
| Interaction | How humans and agents communicate and act together |
| Adaptation | How human and agent behavior changes through interaction |
| Agency | Initiative, autonomy, and independent action |
| Collaboration | Humans and agents accomplishing something together |
| Teaming | Human-agent teams and coordination |
| Delegation | Allocation of action or responsibility |
| Trust | Reliance, confidence, and calibration |
| Dependency | Human reliance on agents |
| Learning | Human and agent learning |
| Training | Development of human-agent capabilities |
| Knowledge exchange | Knowledge transfer between human and agent |
| Memory | Persistence across interactions |
| Companionship | Long-term relational interaction |
| Social influence | How agents affect human behavior |
| Capability | What humans and agents can accomplish or change |
| Embodiment | Physical, virtual, and social embodiment |
| Experience | Human experience of interaction |
| Ethics and society | Consequences beyond individual interaction |
| Multi-agent interaction | Systems containing multiple interacting agents |
| Methodology | How HAI itself should be studied |

This is a **working map**, not a final taxonomy.

# 9. Reciprocal Knowledge and Persistent Interaction

Two additional areas require deeper reconstruction because they intersect strongly with RESEARCH-0005.

### Reciprocal knowledge

The HAI corpus contains explicit work on reciprocal knowledge elicitation for human-agent collaboration.

Therefore the observation:

> “The practitioner possesses knowledge unavailable to the agent.”

is not sufficient to establish a research gap.

The deeper question is whether reciprocal knowledge exchange participates in a **continuously evolving pursuit**, and whether that distinction survives comparison with existing models.

### Persistent interaction

The corpus increasingly includes:

- memory;
- long-term relationships;
- companionship;
- repeated interaction;
- adaptation over time.

Therefore “thinking companion” should be treated as a candidate framing requiring comparison with established terminology rather than as a novel concept.

# 12. Research Discipline

The corpus should be reconstructed in a way that preserves uncertainty.

For every potentially relevant concept:

```text
Observed in corpus
        ↓
Locate original source
        ↓
Determine exact meaning
        ↓
Trace earlier usage
        ↓
Identify competing terminology
        ↓
Identify empirical support
        ↓
Identify contradictions
        ↓
Compare with RESEARCH-0005
```

A concept should not be considered novel merely because:

- the terminology differs;
- the application domain differs;
- the paper uses different theoretical language;
- the concept appears under a neighboring HAI term.

Likewise, conceptual similarity should not be treated as equivalence without examining the underlying definitions.

# 14. Current State

The HAI corpus is a substantial and continuous research ecosystem.

The international conference represents the primary reconstruction target, but the Japanese HAI Symposium beginning in 2006 must be included as its ancestral layer.

The tiered boundary prevents both historical blindness and uncontrolled scope expansion.

The initial map indicates that much of the conceptual territory currently being explored in RESEARCH-0005 already has substantial prior art.

The next task is therefore **not to search for confirmation**.

It is to reconstruct the strongest existing formulations of the relevant concepts and determine whether the current hypotheses:

- are already adequately explained;
- are merely new combinations of established concepts;
- expose tensions between established research programs;
- or survive as genuinely unresolved questions.

The next artifact should therefore move from **mapping the territory** to **deep extraction of the highest-value conceptual clusters**.
